How readings change design

More training did not improve success: fixed-goal 20k fell from 20.0% to 13.6%. Aggressive turn-first control eliminated collisions but left 164 timeouts. Separate safety, progress and arrival instead of adding more batches.

ORIGINAL QUESTION · REWARD DESIGN

“How do PPO's rewards and penalties work at this stage?”

100 × distance_rate − 1
+ 12 × heading_improvement
+ 0.5 × heading_alignment

Arrival earns +500; collision and the then-current 150-step timeout each earn −150. This strongly rewards goal approach and alignment but not increasing obstacle distance. The LiDAR termination rule remains the hard safety limit.

The key change in this progression

Early rewards tried to solve progress, heading, safety and efficiency at once. Later, divide roles: goal controller, LiDAR intervention gate, residual avoidance actor, BC action anchors, curriculum distribution and separate held-out/OOD acceptance.

V1 · CONNECT THE LOOP

5k checkpoint: the interface works; the behavior has not been learned.

Deterministic evaluation hit the pillar after an average 15.6 steps. Actions reached Gazebo, but residual magnitude and policy structure were insufficient for a detour.

First establish that a physically feasible route exists.

Maximum positive steering still collides; maximum negative steering reaches the goal. Zero residual collides at step 30. The old 200-step limit is too short.

V3 · REWARD SHAPING

The direction is right. The action is too weak.

35-step collision

Mean residual is about (−0.219, −0.086). Steering has the expert's sign but is much weaker than the validated [+1, −1]. Reward tuning cannot replace a successful action anchor.

V4 · BEHAVIOR CLONING

Demonstrate once. Then fine-tune with PPO.

648 samples · 1,200 epochs

All 3 expert trajectories succeeded. BC MSE fell from 0.9293 to 0.0000176. Then 5,299 steps produced 24/24 training successes and 10/10 on the final fixed-scene evaluation.

V4 HELD-OUT

Fixed-scene success meets its first generalization test.

59/60 · 98.33%

Only one early pillar-right collision occurred across six unseen small-perturbation scenes. Local generalization was strong, with slight left/right sensitivity.

V5 · CURRICULUM

A 10k-step lateral-pillar curriculum did not improve overall reliability.

10,421 steps · 46/50 train success

Held-out performance stayed at 59/60: the one failure moved from pillar-right to yaw-left. Fixing one case is not necessarily a system improvement.

V5 · ADVERSARIAL OOD

Harder tests bring beautiful numbers back to reality.

43/60 success · 11 collision · 6 timeout

Tasks 06/07 each had 4 collisions; task 02 had 4 timeouts. A 98.33% small-perturbation score does not represent the broader distribution.

V6 · FAILURE MODES

Generate training scenes from failure families while keeping permanent tests isolated.

10,001 steps · 54 episodes

Seed 61129, two scene families alternating 50/50: 31 successes, 15 collisions, 8 timeouts. Training rollouts are not acceptance tests.

V7 → V8 → CLEAN START

Symmetry alone is not enough. Rebuild the data.

Mirror loss 3.20 → 0.19, but OOD success fell to 38.33%

Output symmetry alone lacks a correct action anchor. Switch to successful paired paths, strict mirrored data and 78D temporal LiDAR. Clean-start PPO ultimately scores 29/30 in fair evaluation.

NEURAL NETWORK TAB · ORIGINAL QUESTION

"Why is the neural network 16 dimensions? How do you decide how many dimensions you need?"

The 16 dimensions are neither map coordinates nor evidence that a larger network is smarter. They are the actor's per-step Sensor Summary : obstacle locations, the goal relative to the robot, and the previous action. Exact components are defined by that version's observation-construction code.

Relative observations reduce dependence on a fixed map and transfer more naturally across layouts than absolute coordinates.

01 · TASK VISIBILITY Would removing it make the task unobservable?

Without forward LiDAR the policy cannot anticipate a pillar; without relative goal direction it can avoid obstacles but cannot know where to finish.

02 · REDUNDANCY Is it highly duplicative of the information already available?

Nearly identical adjacent laser beams add redundant inputs. The early 16D summary reduced sample requirements through compression.

03 · MEMORY Can single frames explain motion trends?

A single range snapshot cannot distinguish approaching from receding. Previous actions add continuity; the later 78D input adds range rates to expose motion trends.

04 · GENERALIZATION Do features describe relationships or memorize the scene?

Relative goals, local LiDAR and range rates express transferable relationships. Absolute positions can tie a policy to its training map.

EARLY · 16D Compact observations, sufficient information

Obstacle summaries + relative goal geometry + action history. Easier to train, but compression can discard geometry and temporal trends.

→
CLEAN START · 78D Spatial structure + temporal change

36 LiDAR ranges, 36 range rates, action history and goal information. Failure analysis motivated richer local geometry and temporal context, rather than input size for its own sake.

Principles learned from these experiments

Start with enough information to make the task observable, remove redundancy, and add dimensions when failures show missing information. 16D and 78D answer different stages of the same design question.

For readers starting in robot RL
  • A smoke test validates the pipeline, not the quality of the policy.
  • The success rate must be accompanied by a denominator; both 2/3 and 40/60 are 66.7 per cent, and the intensity of the evidence is quite different.
  • Hybrid policy may be involved in only a few steps, so a small change in overall success rates does not mean that it has no value.
  • Fair comparison needs paired scenes: identical robot starts, pillars and goals for baseline and PPO.
  • Inspect collisions, timeouts, path length and clearance separately; a single reward can hide different failure mechanisms.
Final principles

Training curves describe optimization; frozen Gazebo evaluations describe learned behavior; permanent OOD tests probe unseen environments. Keep these questions separate.
