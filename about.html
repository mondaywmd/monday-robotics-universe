ABOUT MONDAY · ORIGIN STORY

It started
as a vibe.

Then it grew into a robotics universe.

01 / IT’S A VIBE

Two years ago, while working on Stranger Things Season 5 , a phrase suddenly caught on across the team: “It’s a vibe.”

It spread through the chat channels, complete with memes and reactions. Emma, you know I am talking about you—you started this.

Two years later, here I am, starting to vibe my way into this website and a new set of experiments. This time, it is also a way of working: start with a question, build quickly with AI agents, then judge the result against images, data, and physical behavior.

02 / PARALLEL WORLDS

The more I learn about robotics,
the more familiar it feels.

The momentum of the 2023 ChatGPT moment soon reached robotics, simulation, and digital twins. What drew me in was the connection to my own work: VFX and robotics began to look like parallel worlds.

VFX / FILM Build believable digital worlds

Visual realism, physical simulation, procedural generation, complex assets, shot validation, and production pipelines.

↔
ROBOTICS / SIMULATION Build digital worlds robots can learn in

Sensors, dynamics, scene generation, asset semantics, simulation validation, training pipelines, and sim-to-real.

The applications differ, but many of the engineering goals are shared: composable, repeatable, scalable, verifiable systems that make a digital world useful in the real one.

03 / DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE

I was not starting from zero.

At ILM, I use OpenUSD, originally developed and open-sourced by Pixar, and work toward convincing physical simulation and visual results. After more than a decade as a Houdini TD, procedural thinking is a natural part of how I approach problems.

THE SHARED OPERATING SYSTEM

What connects the two fields
is a way of solving problems.

In deadline-driven production, the work rarely arrives as a problem with one correct answer. It calls for judgment, decomposition, validation, and communication. Those habits also matter in robotics experiments.

01 · FRAME Frame the problem

Turn vague feedback into constraints, controllable variables, and acceptance criteria. Check the problem definition before choosing a method.

→
02 · ITERATE Iterate on parameters and strategy

Iteration is more than changing values. Sometimes the method, strategy, or original assumption needs to change.

→
03 · VERIFY Debug and validate quickly

Isolate an issue with a small experiment. Start with a smoke test, then scale up. Use images, logs, and measurements to determine whether a change helped.

→
04 · ALIGN Communicate and iterate

Align goals and limitations with downstream teams, supervisors, and clients, then turn feedback into actionable technical decisions.

Learning quickly, finding the core issue, exercising judgment under pressure, and adapting to new technology are skills I bring from VFX into robotics.

04 / WHAT’S NEXT?

A week of focused study.
A new direction to explore.

I studied NVIDIA OpenUSD Development in my spare time and earned the certification. The question it left me with was not whether I was finished, but: What’s next?

That led to this robotics portfolio: from NavBot colliding with a pillar in Gazebo to PPO, LiDAR, behavior cloning, and TensorBoard; from Blender and AI agents toward Isaac Sim, Omniverse, MuJoCo, Houdini, and simulation-ready assets.

This began as curiosity-driven learning, not a predetermined career pivot. I want to explore what experience in VFX, Houdini, OpenUSD, and production pipelines can contribute to robotics and digital twins.
