DEVLOG · 2026.09.29–30 · VISION TO ACTION

See the litter.
Then approach it.

Previously I supplied a coordinate and tested arrival. Now I want the robot to recognize tissue, milk and water bottles in its own RGB view, locate them and approach them.

Connect real-object references and Blender assets to YOLOE, depth localization and existing PPO navigation: a first step toward a small VLA system.

Jump to three annotated first-person recordings ↓

01 / MOTIVATION

From “go to these coordinates” to “find this object.”

Corridor, speed and living-room tests established some movement capabilities, but I still supplied target coordinates. I wanted to add task meaning: find the trash on the ground and approach it . Navigation now serves a recognizable task, rather than merely completing a trajectory.

Exclude grasping initially. Validate recognition → localization → approach using the tested Gazebo PPO + Isaac route checks, concentrating new uncertainty in vision. Native Isaac Lab PPO remains a separate future training task; no new policy was trained here.

“Small VLA” describes the exploration direction, not a claim about the model architecture. This is a modular vision–localization–action pipeline: YOLOE receives class prompts, detections become navigation coordinates, and PPO proposes motion. It is not an end-to-end model converting arbitrary language and images into actions, and it has no free-form task understanding or grasping.

02 / ASSETS

Turn empty bottles and crumpled tissue into test assets.

I photographed and scanned an empty Meiji 2 L milk jug, an AQUA water bottle and crumpled tissue with Scaniverse. The GLBs help with proportions but contain floor fragments and incomplete transparent surfaces. My decision: Prioritize realism over matching every scanned triangle.

  1. Photos and scans as references: The first version uses approximate dimensions; physical scale calibration has not yet been completed.
  2. Rebuild geometry in Blender: Add bottle shoulders, necks, cap ridges, the milk-jug handle, water-bottle details and labels. Both bottles are empty; no liquid is modeled.
  3. Separate treatment of materials: The milk jug uses milky translucent plastic; the water bottle uses almost transparent thin plastic. Five tissue variants come from folding and deforming continuous sheets, not a physical paper-crumpling simulation.
  4. Import into Isaac Sim: Keep the Blender sources, export USD, textures and simplified colliders, then inspect scale, occlusion and materials through the robot camera.

A USD export bug expanded the milk-jug curve handle to about two meters, blocking the camera. Converting curves to meshes restored the bottle to about 15.5 × 8.1 × 25.1 cm. A good Blender render does not guarantee a usable simulation asset.

Use the approved white robot and revised room lighting. An older scene briefly restored the dark robot and old lights; this was corrected and saved as a separate default appearance layer.

03 / PERCEPTION STACK

Identification gives "What" and depth gives "Where".

01 → RGB Robot's first-person view

Capture RGB and aligned depth from the same camera, saving intrinsics and the camera-to-world transform.

02 → YOLOE Class, bounding box, segmentation mask

YOLOE-11s-seg uses text class prompts to produce labels, confidence scores, boxes and segmentation masks.

03 → DEPTH World Coordinates

Erode the mask boundary and read valid depth. Back-project into camera coordinates, then transform into world coordinates.

04 → GOAL Standoff goal beside the object

Place the approach goal 28 cm toward the robot from the estimated visible surface.

05 → CONTROL PPO + Isaac safety checks

Keep the LiDAR observation adapter and PPO. Depth and LiDAR update the map; the route controller takes over when required.

06 → ACTION Execute v and w, then stop

Differential-drive wheels execute linear and angular velocity. Log arrival, duration, takeovers and obstacle contacts.

YOLOE runs inference with pretrained weights; these assets were not used for retraining in this experiment . All three use the same prompts: milk jug, plastic water bottle, crumpled tissue paper , with a 0.4 detection threshold and deduplication of highly overlapping same-class boxes. The initial prompt milk bottle sometimes missed the object, so I switched to the more shape-specific prompt milk jug and tested again. Official YOLOE documentation ↗

The world position is the median of 3D points inside the detected region. It estimates the visible surface, not the exact object center . Asset placement coordinates do not generate the navigation goal; robot pose still uses simulator ground truth. A missing meter-unit declaration in a new USD layer initially distorted depth scale and was corrected before final capture.

Depth localizes the detected object and also contributes to the existing avoidance map. RGB, YOLOE features and depth images do not enter PPO directly; PPO receives its existing navigation observations and the new goal.

04 / TESTS

One target at a time: connect recognition to action.

With three objects together, watching the robot move does not show which one it recognized. I therefore asked for: Test one target at a time, show annotated first-person views, and separately approach the tissue, milk bottle and water bottle. Reset the scene for each single target. 05 / WHAT WE LEARNED

Reaching the goal does not prove continuous recognition.

Tissue was detected throughout the checked samples; bottles had missed frames during approach. Counts were 22/22 tissue, 20/31 milk and 26/32 water. The denominator includes the departure image and a final-frame recheck, so this is not recall on an independent dataset , and detection was not run on every physics frame.

In an earlier three-object scene, the milk jug was sometimes labeled as a water bottle. Single-target tests and better prompts help, but do not solve viewpoint, lighting or occlusion. Continued arrival relies on the saved initial coordinates, not uninterrupted recognition.

“Trash” is a task definition: I selected known empty bottles and used tissue. Detecting a bottle does not establish that the model knows it is empty, discarded or should be collected. Simulated transparent-bottle depth does not validate real sensor reliability.

An early run moved while recording zero elapsed time: timeline readings did not advance after frame capture. Final timing accumulates PhysX steps; the invalid earlier timing is documented.

2026.10.01 / YOLOE ZERO-SHOT BASELINE

Remember the litter. Find a reachable place to stop.

The changes came from observing the robot: milk bottles, water bottles and tissue all count as litter; a front camera naturally loses sight of a target during a turn; and the robot need not stop at one predetermined point. After three-frame confirmation, the controller now remembers a static world position, chooses a reachable point around a roughly 28 cm standoff ring, and searches outward up to 65 cm if needed. On arrival it turns toward the target and performs a bounded scan.

“Litter” is our task label for known empty bottles and used tissue. The model does not establish whether a bottle is empty. No weights were fine-tuned. Navigation remains PPO plus Isaac route-controller takeover; this is not an end-to-end trained VLA.

The original category-specific, stop-on-visual-loss benchmark passed 2/10 layouts; treating all three categories as litter passed 6/10. This changes task semantics, so it is not a model-accuracy gain. Follow-up tests focused on failed cases 5, 8, 9 and 10: target memory and reachable parking brought all four to an approach point, with three visually confirmed. Case 5 still missed the fallen bottle, motivating the detector comparison below.

These are five fresh orientations × four distances of the same asset in one room, not real-world accuracy or AP. Six negatives hide all litter while retaining books, shoes and the scene; distant views also include the robot. The previous 20-view batch still had three misses with rotations, so 20/20 here does not establish universal reliability. CPU inference is slower. The rotation variant allows 1.2 seconds for static-target visual results; navigation sensor freshness and 12 cm clearance checks remain unchanged.

NEXT / PATROL TO COLLECTION

Patrol from corridor to living room. Find litter, approach it, then resume.

The next application connects the pieces: patrol a corridor-to-living-room route, detect litter along the way, remember confirmed coordinates, select a reachable stopping point, approach and recheck, then resume patrol. Maintain pending, visited and temporarily unreachable target states to avoid repeatedly approaching the same object. Patrol waypoints may be predefined; litter coordinates must come from perception.

Measure objects discovered, false-positive visits, duplicate visits, approach success, time and contact across placements. Record unconfirmed arrivals and unreachable targets separately. A later arm extends the loop to observe → approach → grasp → deposit → verify removal. Reaching an object is not collecting it: grasping adds pose estimation, arm reachability and grasp verification. Patrol and manipulation are not implemented yet; native Isaac Lab PPO training remains a separate direction.
