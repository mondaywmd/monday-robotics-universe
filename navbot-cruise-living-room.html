01 / SPEED

Calibrate straight-line motion before raising cruise speed.

The 0.065 m/s limit helped isolate sensing, planning and contact issues; it was not a measured physical maximum. Increase speed when heading and route permit, using calibration rather than copying a larger Gazebo command.

Issue three-second straight-line commands in the same empty scene. Measure displacement, lateral drift, heading and coasting. Commanded speed is the target; measured mean is actual simulated travel speed.

02 / VISUAL DEBUG

Why does it suddenly turn backwards?

Faster case 2 testing exposed a visibly open forward route while the robot turned back. Wall, mat and PPO hypotheses led to control logging: the safety layer selected an A* turn. Overlay pose, goal, LiDAR, depth, inflated occupancy and planned path to inspect why.

At this moment, the goal is approximately +21.8° , while A* requested a local direction of about −158.0° . The map makes the conflict concrete: a visually open route is marked blocked. The mat collider was inactive here, so it cannot simply explain the turn. Gray is not the robot shape; it is obstacle inflation by 14 cm center clearance. Side points and stale observations can close a visually open gap.

Follow-up testing At the same time, Reduce center clearance to 12 cm and rebuild LiDAR occupancy each scan instead of permanently accumulating points. Case 2 reaches the goal in 21.9 seconds: 102 PPO actions, 7 A* takeovers, sampled force 0 N. Two factors changed, so the result does not isolate either as the cause.

03 / LIVING ROOM

From the sofa to under the desk.

Test more complex furniture and turns in the living room. After initial no-route stops, map-range and sensing adjustments yield a sofa-side arrival in 13.1 seconds: 57 PPO actions, 8 A* takeovers. Start the second route there, targeting the floor beneath the computer desk; move the chair slightly to leave a real gap.

Treat the rug as traversable: disable its collider in the test layer and filter its low depth points. This excludes rug contact from failure checks. Two under-desk attempts stopped for no mapped route; shortening depth-obstacle history cleared stale points at the narrow entrance. The third arrived in Under-desk goal reached in 26.7 seconds , with 90 PPO actions, 32 A* takeovers and sampled contact force 0 N. The first robot-view repeat again reported no mapped route. Restoring the successful 5 Hz capture cadence yielded another 26.7-second arrival. Keep both records: the entrance remains sensitive to map timing, not universally reliable.

The living-room test uses simulator ground-truth pose. The rug is explicitly traversable; physical wheel climbing over its edge was not tested. 0 N means zero in discrete contact-force samples.

05 / NEXT

Next: train a native Isaac PPO policy.

These experiments still use Gazebo-trained PPO weights. Isaac Sim runs inference, mapping and fallback control. Next I want to use Isaac Lab train an independent obstacle-avoidance PPO from scratch, then evaluate it in the same Isaac Sim scenes. Start with reachable corridor goals, without requiring whole-home connectivity reasoning.

Observe relative goal direction/distance, motion state and scans that resolve low obstacles. PPO outputs bounded linear/angular velocity. Progress from one obstacle to shoes, books, dumbbells and random placements; reward arrival/progress and penalize collision, spinning and timeout. Evaluate the new policy alone on unseen layouts against Isaac-only, Gazebo PPO-only and hybrid control for arrival, contacts, time and path.
