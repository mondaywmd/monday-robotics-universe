2026.10.04 / GS · ISAAC SIM · MOCK COLLECTION

Testing litter collection in a new environment

Turn the landing outside my home into a simulation scene, run collection in Isaac Sim, then bring the recorded robot animation into the GS environment.

Watch the result ↓

Result video

A grey wireframe view of the Blender environment reveals the GS scene from left to right. The camera moves gently; robot motion comes from the recorded Isaac Sim run. Physics does not run inside GS.

The workflow

Environment and assets: World Labs generated Gaussian Splatting (GS) from a panorama. Photos and LiDAR informed clean Blender geometry for walls, doors, railings and the wooden table. The environment and tissue, empty milk jug and empty water bottle assets became USD, materials and collision geometry for Isaac Sim.

Isaac Sim collection: Reuse RGB → YOLOE → depth localization, reachable parking and approach, with LiDAR and depth collision avoidance. Remember each static target after multi-frame confirmation, stop at its parking point, then request mock collection. Remove litter only after a successful acknowledgement.

GS presentation: Bring recorded robot animation back into Blender and composite it with native moving-camera GS. Meshes provide robot and litter appearance, ground shadows and wall occlusion. Use water-bottle variant A and reduce exposure by 0.5 stop. Baking GS into mesh textures was unsatisfactory, so GS and meshes remain separate render passes.

Result and scope

This run completed 3/3 mock collections in about 47.2 seconds of simulation time: tissue, water bottle and milk jug. Maximum monitored contact force was 0 N. This is one landing task, not complete patrol coverage, physical grasping or a door-opening demonstration.

GS still blurs or distorts in close views and some directions, and mesh-to-GS alignment is imperfect. For now, use acceptable presentation angles. The chosen version retains railings in the full grey wireframe reveal; tracked, same-frame GS background mattes repair the wall-corner occlusion.
