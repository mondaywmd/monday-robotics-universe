DEVLOG · 2026.09.26 · REPEATABLE NAVIGATION

Change the layout.
Can it still get through?

Starting from the initial 3/8 success baseline, I suspected the mat and clearance margin were changing route choices. Controlled checks led to all eight passage layouts reaching the goal. Jump to my observations and tests ↓

01 / TEST DESIGN

Vary the layout, keep control settings fixed.

Use seeds 2026092601–2026092610. Eight layouts alternate assets along either side, varying order, position and heading within ±10° while preserving a passage. Two controls add a corridor-spanning barrier to test stopping.

These are constrained left/right passage tests, not arbitrary positions and headings. Preparation corrected placement bounds to prevent wall intersections. Aborted preparation runs are excluded; no settings changed or failures were removed during the formal ten tests.

Keep depth + horizontal LiDAR, floor fitting, 2.5 cm grid and A*. Inflation radius: 17.5 cm; linear limit: 0.065 m/s; angular limit: 0.55 rad/s. Pose is ground truth, depth idealized and obstacles static.

02 / RESULTS

Report arrival and stopping separately.

Open passages: 3/8 reached the goal and met contact and braking criteria. Blocked controls: 2/2 met the specified stop criteria. Both blocked cases also planned behind the start before the no-progress stop. Meeting the stop rule does not prove correct blockage recognition; do not call these goal arrivals or combine all ten as navigation successes.

Ten complete videos, including failures.

04 / WHAT WE LEARNED

One successful run does not establish reliability.

Case 2 initially approached the goal, then drove behind the start and stopped for no progress without detected contact. Investigate mapping and route choice; an unpushable shoe is not the explanation, and video alone does not identify a sensor parameter as the cause.

Case 7 also stopped beside the corridor at X ≈ −1.81 m. Unknown space was treated as traversable, old obstacle points persisted, and depth timestamps were not independently checked. Preserve this baseline; log maps, plans and floor-fit quality to isolate causes on the same seed.

05 / OBSERVER'S NOTES

Look at the trajectory, make the judgment, then test it with a comparison.

The initial 3/8 result prompted observation: case 2 turned back immediately, while case 7 took a side room near the chair despite resembling successful case 3. I proposed two tests: the mat might register as an obstacle, and 17.5 cm clearance might close a usable passage.

Observation 1: the mat may block case 2.

The mat lay in the intended detour. Its 2.1 cm height exceeded the 1.2 cm depth threshold. Disable only that raised geometry in a case 2 copy, keeping seed, objects, goal and settings. The original turned back and stopped at 18.3 seconds; without the mat it arrived at 65.9 seconds without detected contact. This supports the mat hypothesis for that run. The pale floor texture remains.

Observation 2: 17.5 cm clearance was too conservative.

Case 7 still turned into the side room with the mat present. Logged plans showed deliberate leftward routing before the doorway. The horizontal robot radius is about 10.5 cm; 17.5 cm center clearance adds about 7 cm. At 29.7 seconds, the best corridor-only route offered about 15.2 cm center clearance, so the threshold excluded it.

Change only minimum center clearance to 14 cm, keeping the mat and layout. Case 7 reaches the goal in 66.9 seconds without detected collision. This leaves about 3.5 cm beyond the horizontal bounding circle; it is a tested simulation setting, not a hardware recommendation.

Save the changes, repeat all ten layouts.

Remove the raised mat for subsequent scenes, save the 14 cm setting and rerun the same ten seeds. Passage arrival improves from 3/8 to 8/8; both blocked controls stop. All sampled forces meet the threshold. No-progress stopping does not establish dead-end understanding.
