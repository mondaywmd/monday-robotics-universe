2026.10.27 / C2 / REAL-ROBOT DEMO

One click to fetch a red cube

From five manual steps to one click: observe, plan, approach, restore the arm pose, then reacquire and grasp. A real-robot run shown through the control panel and a synchronized phone recording.

1. Observe2. Locate + plan3. Approach4. Restore grasp pose5. Reacquire + grasp

From a stationary grasp to a complete mobile task.

In the previous stage, the robot could localize and grasp a red cube while stationary. This time, I wanted to extend the task: look for the cube, locate it, calculate an approach, drive to a suitable parking point, switch the arm pose, and grasp.

That used to mean five separate button presses. “Fetch Red Cube” now connects the stages: observe → locate and plan → approach and park → restore the grasp pose → reacquire and grasp. The video shows a successful real-robot approach, grip, lift and release. For now, searching means inspecting the camera’s visible scene, not autonomous whole-home patrol.

Repair the foundations, then connect the workflow.

A substantial part of the work was diagnosing the original low-level IMU communication and reading problems: correcting I²C read timing, adding validity diagnostics, and handling bias compensation with reuse across boots. That let us continue validating base motion without restarting from a manual calibration every time. Stationary heading drift and bias consistency between boots still need validation; one successful run does not mean every IMU issue is resolved.

Integration was another challenge. Functions that worked separately exposed mismatches in pose references, completion states, sampling handoffs and failure recovery when chained together. We progressively aligned those handoffs, reused resident sampling and driving services, and moved the complete task onto the robot. The web panel starts the task and displays progress; a browser heartbeat is no longer required to keep the robot’s task running.

After parking, the robot restores the grasp pose and acquires a fresh RGB-D pair for close-range grasp planning, rather than blindly reusing the earlier distant observation.

Next: make it faster and extend the grasping task.

  • Speed and efficiency: real driving is still fairly slow. Improve travel speed, waiting time and program handoffs. The 34-second video is an accelerated edit, not the actual task duration.
  • More objects: bring in the YOLOE perception work from our earlier experiments and extend beyond the red cube to dumbbells, tissues and Crocs shoes—the objects we called “trash” in those tests. Each also needs its own validation of depth, grasp points and grasp strategy.
  • More complex paths: integrate MoveIt 2 when the arm needs an obstacle-avoiding path. More complex routes for the mobile base require a separate navigation planner.

The milestone is turning a stationary grasp into a mobile grasping workflow started with one click. This successful field demonstration is a step forward; more placements and scenes will test how reliably it repeats.
