2026.10.01 / PATROL · YOLOE · MOCK COLLECTION

Patrol. Find litter. Simulate collection.

Two synchronized views of one run: robot RGB with online YOLOE detections, and an observer camera showing approach, mock pickup acknowledgement and resumed patrol.

Objects disappear when the mock collector returns success. There is no arm and no physical grasping in this experiment.

01 / MOTIVATION

Why simulate the pickup module first?

I wanted to move beyond approaching one visible object and toward an application: patrol from the corridor into the living room, discover litter and move on to the next item. My proposal was to isolate collection as a module: after successful docking, send a mock “collected” acknowledgement so the full task flow can run. A future arm would replace that acknowledgement with the outcome of grasping and depositing the object.

02 / THE LOOP

A living room with multiple pieces of litter.

The scene contains eight litter items: one in the corridor and seven in the living room, mixing empty milk bottles, upright and fallen water bottles, and crumpled tissue. The approved white robot and room lighting are retained. The rug is treated as traversable, while furniture remains part of obstacle avoidance. Patrol waypoints are predefined; litter coordinates come from RGB → YOLOE → depth localization.

After three stable observations, the robot stores the target’s world position. Losing sight of it during a turn does not erase the task; newly detected targets enter a queue. Parking is selected from reachable positions around the object. Once docked and stationary, the robot requests mock collection. Only a successful acknowledgement removes that item and marks it complete, preventing repeat visits. Near-arrival visual reconfirmation is recorded separately and is not required by this mock pickup contract.

03 / RESULTS

Collection success and patrol completion are separate.

The initial run mock-collected 8/8 items by about 120.1 seconds, then stopped at 173.6 seconds when a route back to an old patrol waypoint became unavailable. This demonstrated the multi-target collection flow, not successful full patrol coverage. The observer view required a new run; the table and video report that recorded run’s own results rather than reusing the earlier numbers.

Experimental scope and implementation notes

YOLOE uses pretrained weights with rotation-assisted inference, without fine-tuning. Navigation remains PPO plus Isaac route takeover. An early patrol-resumption attempt made contact; the short swept-path check was then applied to every executed action, including route fallback, with 12 cm body clearance plus a 2 cm planning margin. Robot localization still uses simulator ground truth. The mock collector matches scene identity only to remove the object; that identity does not generate navigation goals. Transparent-bottle depth is simulated and does not validate real sensor behavior. This is not an end-to-end trained VLA.

04 / NEXT

Make the task complete, then make it physical.

Next, improve task scheduling: after collection, select a useful reachable patrol continuation, reduce backtracking and record temporarily unreachable areas. Then test different layouts, occlusions and negative examples. An arm would add grasping, depositing and removal verification in place of the mock collector.
