DEVLOG · 2026.09.23 · REALITY CAPTURE

From a real room
to an editable digital environment.

Compare photographs, Scaniverse LiDAR and reconstruction of the same space. Test whether an AI agent can turn captured reality into editable assets for robotics simulation.

01 / THE QUESTION

Scans capture reality,
but not a clean room asset.

Scaniverse supplies scale, spatial relationships and an 8192×8192 texture, but also noise, broken boundaries, occlusion and baked lighting. Photos add visual cues without reliable depth. Combine both sources instead of asking either to solve the entire reconstruction.

02 / WORKFLOW

Six steps turn reality collection into a Blender scene where work can continue.

  1. 01
    Photo reference

    Understand the space before modeling.

    Organize 15 HEIC photos into JPGs and previews; identify corridors, openings, furniture, lights, frames and unseen regions. Photos guide appearance, not exact measurement.

  2. 02
    Scaniverse LiDAR

    Scans are used as measuring evidence.

    Import the GLB with scan coordinates and metric scale. Sample triangle normals, centers and areas to fit walls, floor and ceiling. Corridor width at Y=0 is about 1.089 m; reconstructed clear height is about 2.60 m.

  3. 03
    Protected reference

    The original scans are only for reference purposes and are not quietly altered.

    Hide the original scan in a Reference Collection and verify unchanged geometry, topology and UV hashes. Save architecture, photo-based and capture versions separately.

  4. 04
    Clean reconstruction

    Rebuild clean, modular architecture.

    Build walls, openings, floor, ceiling, frames and skirting. The first architectural version has 22 new mesh components, 368 vertices and 324 quad faces. Inferred walls are separate and marked uncertain.

  5. 05
    Texture & lighting recovery

    Transfer real appearance onto editable surfaces.

    Perspective-correct photos and scan textures guide flooring and walls; restore lights, vents, decorations and furniture. The capture method records indirect diffuse appearance through camera rays, not fully relightable PBR materials.

  6. 06
    Compare & validate

    Compare structure, appearance and downstream usability.

    Render simplified, raw-scan and texture-capture versions from the same view. Check finite coordinates, packed images, ceiling visibility and source hashes. Judge editability, reproducibility and exportability as well as appearance.

03 / EVIDENCE

Geometry, photographs and Lidar, each answering different questions.

04 / GAUSSIAN SPLATTING TEST

Scaniverse Gaussian Splatting:
Fast visual capture, but unsuitable as a final simulation asset.

Import all 525,679 Scaniverse Gaussian splats into Blender. Colors and atmosphere survive, but the narrow corridor, white walls, occlusion and exposure changes expose clear limitations.

GAUSSIAN SPLATTING Useful for quick visual previews

It preserves lighting and color, but lacks explicit wall, door, floor and furniture components. Collision, semantics, physics materials and independent randomization are difficult to assign directly.

LiDAR MESH Useful as a dimensional reference

Provides metric scale, major planes and rough geometry, but also contains scan noise, holes, occlusion and complex triangulation.

LiDAR-GUIDED BLENDER SCENE The selected approach

Use LiDAR to constrain scale and spatial relationships, then rebuild clean, separate Blender components. Photos and scan textures guide materials and lighting.

05 / FINAL CONCLUSION

Selected approach: use LiDAR as geometric evidence,
reassemble the room from separate 3D modules.

Separate components

Separate walls, doors, floors, ceilings and furniture make editing, replacement, labeling and domain randomization easier.

Low geometry noise

Clean planes and clear topology are better suited to collision geometry, navigation and physics validation than scan noise or splats.

Reality anchored

LiDAR constrains scale and spatial relationships; photos and captured textures help reproduce materials, colors and lighting.

Sim-ready path

Modular assets make it easier to add collision proxies, semantics and material parameters, then export to OpenUSD, Isaac Sim or other robotics platforms.

Reconstruction-stage limits (September 23): This is not a calibrated building survey or a fully validated simulation asset. Wall thicknesses, room depth, furniture and unseen regions include plausible reconstruction. It nevertheless provides clearer, more controllable structure than raw scans or splats.

06 / SIM-READY HANDOFF

Visual reconstruction is complete.
A simulation-ready asset needs more.

Once navigation was the target, separate visual and collision models. Isaac Sim was not yet installed on September 23, so deliver a portable USD package, scripts, dependencies and import instructions rather than claiming runtime validation.

01 · SOURCE SAFETY Preserve the visual master

Preserve the photo-based and Blender visual versions; create a separate Isaac Sim export.

02 · TRANSFORMS Ground from Z = 0.08 to zero

Move the entire Sim-ready scene level or root node in a uniform manner, not just the ground, to avoid the furniture being suspended and collisions being misplaced.

03 · GEOMETRY Separate visual and collision geometry

Keep detailed visual meshes; use simple, stable collision proxies for walls, floors, doorways and furniture.

04 · MATERIALS Convert Blender-specific nodes

Bake complex shaders into portable Base Color, Roughness, Metallic and Normal textures.

05 · PORTABILITY Use relative paths in USD

Ship textures, USD layers and dependencies together using relative paths.

06 · VALIDATION DEBT Record what remains unverified

Record units, up axis, bounding box, component count and missing textures; validate physics and sensors later in Isaac Sim.

09 / PHYSICS SANITY TEST · 2026.09.24

From opening a file to
verifying floor contact.

Run Isaac Sim 6.1.0 natively on Windows, import the Blender corridor USD and test gravity and floor contact with a cube: the first runtime environment check.

01 / SETUP

Validate the smallest experiment first

Use meters, Z-up and separate Visual/Collision layers. The cube has a gravity-enabled, non-kinematic rigid body and convex hull; the floor uses a static triangle mesh.

02 / OBSERVED

Local floor-contact test passed

Select a location within the corridor where the cube can drop and park on the floor. Then try the initial speed and material setting, observe motion and contact. This proves that the basic physical interaction of the position is available.

03 / OPEN QUESTION

Initial penetration still needs investigation

The first drop penetrated the floor; a different corridor location worked. Collision coverage, alignment and initial height at the original point remain unresolved. This does not validate the entire floor.

What's done this time? What's left?

Isaac Sim installation, room import and local rigid-body/collider checks are complete. Next investigate penetration, import NavBot URDF/meshes, and inspect links, joints, mass/inertia and collisions. Navigation, sensors, ROS2, Isaac Lab, RL and Gazebo comparisons remain unvalidated in this scene at this stage.

10 / PHYSICS MATERIAL · 2026.09.24

Assign a physics material,
and make it bounce on landing.

After floor contact, create and bind Cube_PhysicsMaterial and increase restitution. The green cube visibly bounces: the experiment moves from valid collision to adjusting contact behavior.

01 / CREATE

Create a Rigid Body Material

Create → Physics → Physics Material; enable Rigid Body Material and name it Cube_PhysicsMaterial.

02 / BIND

Bind appearance and physics separately.

Select Cube and bind the new material under Physics materials on selected models, keeping OmniPBR for appearance. The shader controls green color; the physics material controls friction and bounce.

03 / TUNE

Parameters in this record

Static Friction: 0.4
Dynamic Friction: 0.3
Restitution: 0.8
Friction Combine Mode: average
Restitution Combine Mode: max

Appearance preserved; bounce clearly visible.

Material binding and restitution changed post-contact motion. This is a qualitative demonstration, without rebound-height measurement or real-material calibration. The earlier penetration issue remains unresolved.
